## Abstract

Photovoltaic manufacturing is organized as a multi-tier production chain with different capital intensities and loading constraints across polysilicon, wafers, cells, and modules. During 2022-2025, this chain experienced rapid capacity growth, persistent underutilization, and sharp price compression. We estimate comparable Cournot oligopoly models for the four major tiers of the China-centered crystalline-silicon supply chain, recovering tier-specific demand elasticities, utilization-dependent marginal-cost schedules, and markups. The estimated cost curvature differs markedly across tiers: wafers and cells exhibit U-shaped schedules with interior cost minima, polysilicon marginal cost declines monotonically, and module marginal cost declines approximately linearly. We interpret these differences as reflecting distinct forms of operational rigidity along the chain. Counterfactuals show that an upstream marginal-cost wedge is amplified at the polysilicon-to-wafer link because selective exit raises concentration, but attenuates farther downstream. A three-rung merger ladder further shows that the welfare effects of consolidation depend on the operational channel: common control reallocates output across separate plants, direct efficiency transfer changes the pair’s cost-side efficiency, and full integration pools capacity and shifts the merged producer along the estimated utilization-cost schedule.
