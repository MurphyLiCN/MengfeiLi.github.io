## Chinese Abstract

在全球政治经济形势不断变化的背景下，供应链韧性成为产业界与学术界高度关注的研究议题。本文针对现有韧性度量方法的局限，回应对非概率化的、涵盖多方面韧性特征的统一动态指标的需求，提出两种非参数供应链韧性评估指标——结构变点（Structural Change-Points, SCP）与加权累计损失（Weighted Cumulative Loss, WCL），对韧性度量工具箱进行补充与贡献。SCP基于变点检测技术，用以识别供应链因外部冲击引发的结构性变化；WCL则利用动态权重函数，量化冲击导致的绩效损失。两者共同刻画供应链在“吸收—适应—恢复”全周期下的表现，弥补传统方法的不足。在实证应用中，本研究利用中美海关数据，以2018年中美贸易战与2020年新冠疫情为外部冲击案例，检验了该指标体系的可用性。研究度量了不同产品供应链的韧性差异，深入分析了供应网络结构对韧性的影响。结果显示，局部冲击下，拥有多元化的供应网络和分散化的客户基础，能增强供应链的韧性；而系统冲击下，链接的“强度”比“广度”更有利于韧性。本文构建的指标框架具备适用性与推广价值，可为企业提升供应链风险管理能力提供理论支持与决策工具。

## English Abstract

In the context of a constantly evolving global political and economic landscape, supply chain resilience has emerged as a topic of significant interest within both industry and academia. Addressing the limitations of existing resilience metrics and the demand for a unified, non-probabilistic dynamic indicator that encompasses multiple facets of resilience, this paper proposes two non-parametric indicators for supply chain resilience assessment: Structural Change-Points (SCP) and Weighted Cumulative Loss (WCL). These indicators are intended to complement and contribute to the existing toolkit for resilience measurement. The SCP indicator, based on change-point detection techniques, is designed to identify structural shifts in the supply chain caused by external shocks. The WCL indicator, conversely, quantifies performance losses resulting from disruptions by employing a dynamic weighting function. Together, these indicators characterize the performance of a supply chain throughout the entire “absorb-adapt-recover” cycle, overcoming the deficiencies of traditional methods. For empirical validation, this study utilizes customs data from China and the United States to test the applicability of this indicator framework, with the 2018 US-China trade war and the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic serving as case studies of external shocks. The research measures the differential resilience of various product supply chains and conducts an in-depth analysis of the impact of supply network structure on resilience. The findings indicate that under localized shocks, a diversified supply network and a decentralized customer base enhance supply chain resilience. Under systemic shocks, however, the “strength” of the network proves to be more critical than its “breadth.” The indicator framework constructed in this paper demonstrates both applicability and potential for broader generalization, offering theoretical support and decision-making tools for enterprises seeking to improve their supply chain risk management capabilities.
