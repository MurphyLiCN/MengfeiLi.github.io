## Abstract

Despite its growing cultural salience, little-treat consumption remains theoretically uncharted in the consumer behavior literature. Hedonic choice and hedonic choice literatures focus on higher-stakes indulgences and self-control conflicts that do not capture the proactive, budget-conscious logic of everyday micro-indulgences. This study addresses these gaps through an AI-human hybrid, multi-method design integrating grounded-theory qualitative discovery, human survey validation, and GLLM-based robustness testing. The results show that: (1) little-treat consumption is a hedonic self-regulatory strategy in which consumers deliberately deploy low-cost, accessible consumption acts, authorized through self-licensing cognition, to achieve rapid mood repair and affirm personal worth, and is empirically distinguishable from impulse buying, self-gifting, compensatory consumption, and materialism; (2) the construct comprises four core dimensions: Emotion-Triggering, Immediate Gratification, Low-Cost Accessibility, and Self-Reward/Self-Care, captured by a validated 12-item scale with strong psychometric properties across both pilot and main human survey samples; (3) the scale is further validated through synthetic respondent data generated by three frontier GLLMs with no item migration, providing cross-method structural convergence; (4) systematic inter-factor correlation inflation across all GLLM-generated datasets reveals a motivational complexity that synthetic data can approximate but only lived experience fully captures. These findings advance construct conceptualization of micro-hedonic behavior under economic constraint, establish a replicable AI-human hybrid workflow for construct development and scale validation, and offer actionable implications for brand positioning and marketing communication strategies targeting treat-oriented consumer segments.
